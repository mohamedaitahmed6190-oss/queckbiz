When should you issue a receipt?

Issue a receipt any time money changes hands — whether the payment is cash, card, or bank transfer. Receipts matter most for cash sales, where no bank record exists to prove the transaction, and for deposits or partial payments, where the customer needs written proof of what they have already paid and how much is still owed.

  • Give the receipt immediately at the point of payment, while the customer is still with you.
  • Keep a copy for your own records — receipts are the backbone of accurate bookkeeping and tax filing.
  • Number your receipts sequentially so they are easy to reconcile at year-end.
  • Always note the payment method (cash, card, transfer) in case a charge is later disputed.
  • For a deposit or partial payment, state the amount paid and the remaining balance clearly.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between a receipt and an invoice?
An invoice requests payment before or after delivery, while a receipt confirms that payment has already been received. A receipt closes the transaction; an invoice creates the payment obligation. Read our detailed comparison.

Is this receipt maker free?
Yes, it's completely free with no signup, and everything is generated locally in your browser.

Do I need to give a receipt for every sale?
Requirements vary by country and business type, but it's good practice to provide a receipt for any payment so both sides have a record for bookkeeping and tax purposes.

Can I use this for a cash payment?
Yes. Simply select the payment method as cash when filling in the receipt details.

Should a receipt include tax?
If the sale is taxable, show the tax amount separately so the customer has a clear record and your own bookkeeping stays accurate at filing time.

Can I issue a receipt for a deposit or partial payment?
Yes. Note the amount received and the balance still due, so both sides have a clear record of what remains to be paid.

How long should I keep copies of receipts?
Most tax authorities recommend keeping business records for several years — commonly three to seven, depending on your country. Saving a PDF of each receipt makes this effortless.

Is a sales receipt different from a regular receipt?
They are essentially the same document — a sales receipt simply emphasises that it records a completed sale. Both confirm payment was received and itemise what it covered.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Issuing a receipt before the money has actually arrived.
  • Not stating the payment method (cash, card, transfer).
  • Missing date or unique receipt number, so the payment cannot be traced later.
  • Using a receipt when the client needs an invoice with tax details.

Good to know

Keep a copy of every receipt you issue. In most countries, business records must be kept for several years, and the exact period differs between Morocco, France and elsewhere, so check with your accountant.
