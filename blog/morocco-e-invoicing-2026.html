Morocco E-Invoicing 2026: What Has Been Announced and How to Prepare

By Mohamed Ait-Ahmed·October 18, 2026·6 min read

Electronic invoicing is one of the most closely followed tax topics in Morocco in 2026. The information circulating from the press, software vendors and firms does not always agree on dates and thresholds. This article summarizes what emerges from several sources, flags what remains uncertain and explains what it means for a small business that creates invoices as PDFs.

Important: the timelines below are indicative and come from secondary sources. Only the official texts and announcements of the Direction Générale des Impôts (DGI) are authoritative. Confirm your situation with your accountant.

What has been announced

  • Legal basis: Article 145 of the General Tax Code, reinforced by the 2024 finance law, provides for the use of an invoicing system meeting technical criteria set by the administration.
  • "Clearance" model: according to several sources, each invoice would be validated by a national DGI platform before being passed to the customer.
  • Structured formats: XML formats (UBL and CII are mentioned) would be expected, with an electronic signature. A plain PDF, even signed, would not be enough on its own.

The timeline as reported

According to articles published in 2026, the rollout would be gradual: large companies first, then mid-sized companies, then SMEs, very small businesses and auto-entrepreneurs, with a 500,000 MAD turnover threshold often cited for the smallest, and deadlines running from 2027 to beyond 2028. These sources themselves say the dates are "indicative" and may change. Do not base any decision on a date without checking it on the official sites.

What it means for your PDF invoices

If your business falls within scope, an invoice created in a simple generator and sent by email will no longer be enough: you will need a compliant invoicing system connected to the DGI platform. Our invoice generator is not a certified e-invoicing system; it produces a PDF or printed document. It remains useful for quotes, delivery notes, proformas, receipts, preparing and tracking your documents, and for businesses not yet affected.

How to prepare now

  1. Identify your category (status, turnover) with your accountant to know whether and when you will be affected.
  2. Clean up your data: correct ICE, tax ID and RC for you and your customers (see the checklist of mandatory details).
  3. Keep continuous numbering (see how to number your invoices).
  4. Archive documents properly; the legal retention period should be confirmed with your accountant (see organizing business documents).
  5. Follow official DGI communications (tax.gov.ma) rather than commercial summaries.

Questions to ask your accountant

  • Is my business affected by e-invoicing, and from what date?
  • Will my current invoicing software be compatible, or will I need to change?
  • Do I go through an administration platform, an approved provider, or both?
  • How will credit notes, corrective invoices and foreign-currency invoices be handled?
  • What are the penalties or consequences of being late to comply?

Frequently asked questions

Is e-invoicing already mandatory for everyone?

No. According to the sources consulted the rollout is gradual, but dates vary between articles. Check with the DGI or your accountant.

Will a signed PDF be enough?

According to several sources, no: a structured format validated by the platform is expected.

Can I keep using your generator?

Yes for documents outside the scope and for preparing your data, as long as you are not affected. If you are, use a compliant solution.
